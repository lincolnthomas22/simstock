How to play

Everything in SimStock is made up: the companies, the prices and the money. Nothing here can cost you anything, so the worst that can happen is you learn something.

is one trading day

  1. 1

    Find your way around

    The bar across the top of every room is how you move about. Home takes you back to Mr. Sim, and the buttons on the right jump straight to any room. The room you're in is the green one.

    • 1 Back to the home screen, where the portfolio and this guide live.
    • 2 The room you are in now.
  2. 2

    Open a brokerage account

    A brokerage account is what lets you buy shares. On the front page, press the gold Open an account button. You'll get three short cards explaining what a share is, then the account opens and you land on the trading floor with five companies to choose from.

    • 1 Press here. You only do this once.
  3. 3

    Read the trading floor

    This is where you'll spend most of your time. Pick a company on the left, study it in the middle, and buy or sell on the right.

    • 1 The watchlist. Every company, grouped by the account tier that unlocks it. Click one to look at it. Greyed-out names are ones you can watch but not yet trade.
    • 2 Today's price and how much it moved since yesterday. Green is up, red is down.
    • 3 The chart. Switch between one month, three months and a year. Hover it to see the price on any day. The gold dashed line is what you paid, so anything above it is profit.
    • 4 The facts about the company, each with a plain-English note under it.
    • 5 The order ticket, where you buy and sell.
    • 6 Your shares in this company, and whether you are up or down on them.
  4. 4

    Place your first order

    Orders fill instantly at the price on screen. There are no fees in SimStock, so a trade costs exactly the price times the number of shares.

    • 1 Choose Buy or Sell.
    • 2 Type how many shares, or use the minus and plus buttons.
    • 3 Max fills in as many as your cash allows, or when selling, everything you own.
    • 4 Press the button to place the order. If it is greyed out, the note underneath says why, usually not enough cash.

    Watch Cash afterwards. Spending every last dollar leaves you nothing to buy with when a price drops.

  5. 5

    Make money three ways

    There is no single right way to play, but money reaches you through three doors.

    • Selling for more than you paid. Your gain is only "on paper" until you sell. Sell, and it becomes cash you keep. That is the difference between the Gain or loss and Profit from sales lines.
    • Dividends. Some companies pay their shareholders cash every quarter, roughly once a minute. Hold the shares and it lands in your account by itself. The dividend yield stat tells you who pays.
    • Staff, minus their wages. Staff bring in client fees every trading day, and draw wages every day. Fees rise and fall with the market. Wages don't. In a good stretch they make you money, and in a bad one they cost you, so keep cash aside. If you can't cover the wages, someone gets let go.

    Watch the Staff, net figure at the top of the screen. Green means your desk pays for itself. Red means it's eating your cash.

  6. 6

    Check your portfolio

    The Portfolio room adds everything up: your net worth, how much of it is cash and how much is in shares, every company you hold, and a set of bars showing how your money is split. If one bar is much longer than the rest, most of your fortune is riding on one company.

    • 1 Click any row to open that company on the trading floor.
  7. 7

    Grow the firm in Upgrades

    The Upgrades room has the two things that make your desk bigger.

    • Account tiers. Starter, Silver, Gold and Platinum. Each one needs a level and a one-off fee, and each unlocks four more companies: seventeen in all, from a water utility up to mining, airlines and robotics.
    • Staff. A Research Analyst costs $50 to hire, brings in $1 a day in fees and draws $0.45 a day in wages. Each hire costs a little more than the last, and better roles unlock with better accounts. The page tells you how clients are feeling, which is what decides the fees.

    Your level is the bar at the top of the screen. You earn XP by trading, hiring and collecting dividends, and every level pays a cash bonus.

  8. 8

    Things worth knowing

    • Your progress saves by itself, in this browser. Using another device or clearing your browsing data starts you over. Settings, at the top right, can reset the game on purpose.
    • Prices move on their own every second, and every company reports earnings each quarter. Good news usually lifts a price and bad news drops it, but not always.
    • Spread your money out. Owning several companies means one bad earnings report cannot wipe you out. The index fund, IDXF, holds a slice of the whole market and is the calmest place to start.
    • High volatility cuts both ways. Voltaic Motors, Helix Therapeutics and Velocity Airways can jump or fall by a tenth in a day. Keep those positions small until you are comfortable.
    • Three companies can fail completely: Voltaic Motors, Helix Therapeutics and Velocity Airways. If one loses most of its value, it posts a warning that it may not be able to pay its debts. From then on it might collapse on any day, and if it does, the shares are worthless and trading in it closes for good. The money does not come back. That is the price of the bigger swings, and it is why nobody sensible puts everything into one wild company.

That's everything. The market is open whenever you are.
